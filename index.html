Court Judgement Details

The Seoul Central District Court sided with ADOR and against NewJeans on October 30, 2025. This page provides the summarised details about the Court's Ruling.
This page may have incorrect information as it was written by a single person. Check the information given here.

Main Point

1. Min Hee-Jin

NewJeans argued that the dismissal of Min Hee Jin broke trust between ADOR.

The Court:

  • The contract does not require Min Hee-Jin to be an exclusive produer/remain as CEO.
  • The contract did not contain a "keyman clause".
  • Min Hee-Jin's participation in selection of NewJeans' members is unproven.
  • Even after Min Hee-Jin got dismissed as CEO, ADOR offered her a producer role for NewJeans, which Min Hee-Jin rejected.
  • Min Hee-Jin was trying to break ADOR from HYBE. Therefore, the actions of Min Hee-Jin lead to her dismissal.
    • Min Hee-Jin contacted outside investors and other people and discussed how to operate independently.
    • Min Hee-Jin was assisted by a Shaman with the process of operating ADOR independently.

2. HYBE

The Court also said that HYBE is the core foundation of NewJeans:

  • HYBE invested about 21b KRW ($14.4m) into NewJeans.
  • HYBE gave ADOR resources.
  • HYBE established ADOR solely for NewJeans.
  • HYBE recognised the trainees of NewJeans.

3. Contract Binding

NewJeans argued the destruction of trust was enough to terminate the contract.

The Court:

  • The Court said that a contract cannot be terminated because of one party's emotions.
  • The contract between NewJeans and ADOR had a strict clause (Article 15) because ADOR invested a lot into NewJeans pre-debut. If NewJeans would've
    terminated their contract early, ADOR would've suffered irrepairable damages and lose investors.
  • NewJeans claimed they were not aware of Article 15. However, the Court rejected the arguement, noting that they negotiated with
    ADOR for each clause.

Those were the main points argued by NewJeans' lawyers. I'll add the other points later if I can.
